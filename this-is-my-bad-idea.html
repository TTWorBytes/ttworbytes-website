THE STORY

Six Challenges. One Very Bad Idea. What Could Possibly Go Wrong?

A comic challenge story about courage, pride, friendship and discovering that being brave is not the same as proving yourself.

Jake agrees to a series of six challenges designed to find out whether he is really as brave as he likes to think he is. Jack takes on the job of keeping everything fair and organised.

The challenges push Jake well outside his comfort zone: scorching curry, a memory test, a penalty shoot-out, poetry, a headstand and wrestling. Each one sounds manageable until Jake is the person who actually has to do it.

With Alex, Ruby and Jack beside him, Jake becomes increasingly determined not to give up. But somewhere between courage and pride, he begins to discover that proving yourself can be a very bad idea.

AT THE HEART OF THIS STORY

The Boy Keeping Score

Jake may be the one doing the challenges, but Jack is the person making sure his twin follows the rules.

“You agreed to the challenge. I'm only making sure you do it properly.”

Jack has recovered enough to join the family holiday, although he still tires more easily than he once did. To Jake, simply having his brother beside him makes every ordinary moment feel important.

As the mystery unfolds, the brothers explore the town, follow clues and rediscover the easy closeness they had before illness changed everything.

The adventure may begin with a message in a bottle, but the real treasure is the chance for the family to feel whole again.

Meet the People

WHY THIS STORY MATTERS

Brave—or Just Stubborn?

At first, the challenges look like a chance for Jake to show everyone what he can do. Naturally, Jake thinks this is an excellent reason to say yes.

But every challenge asks something different of him. Some require nerve, some concentration, some physical effort—and some simply require Jake to stop panicking long enough to think.

Alex brings his usual humour, Ruby knows more than most about genuine strength, and Jack refuses to let Jake change the rules whenever things become uncomfortable.

Underneath the comedy, the question is simple: does courage mean never backing down, or can the braver decision sometimes be knowing when you have nothing left to prove?
